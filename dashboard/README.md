# Dashboard

Built in Tableau Public from the CSVs in `dashboard/data/`. **Live version:** _link coming soon_

Screenshots of each page are in `screenshots/`.

## Data files

| File | Grain | Used on |
|---|---|---|
| `overview_kpis.csv` | one row | Overview |
| `rfm_segment_summary.csv` | one row per segment | Segments |
| `cohort_retention_long.csv` | cohort week × weeks since first purchase | Retention |
| `category_performance.csv` | one row per category | Products |
| `price_volume_by_article.csv` | one row per article (10+ units) | Products |
| `price_quintile_summary.csv` | category × price quintile | Products |
| `price_correlation_by_category.csv` | one row per category | Products, Limits |

`overview_kpis.csv` and `price_volume_by_article.csv` are generated by `python scripts/build_tableau_extracts.py`. The rest are built from `outputs/summary_tables/`.

All spend and price fields end in `_scaled`. They are anonymized price units, never currency. Don't apply a currency number format to them in Tableau.

## Page plan

### 1. Overview
- Tiles: customers, transactions, articles sold, date range (from `overview_kpis.csv`).
- Subtitle on the page itself: *"50% random sample of customers active Jun 22 to Sep 22, 2020. Prices are anonymized and scaled, not currency."*

### 2. Customer segments
- Horizontal bar: `customers` by `segment`, sorted descending. Lost should sit on top.
- Second bar or a dual measure: `pct_of_customers` next to `pct_of_spend`, so Champions (11.5% of customers, 33.5% of spend) stand out.
- Tooltip: `rule`, `median_recency_days`, `median_purchase_days`.
- Headline text: *"A third of customers had lapsed within three months. Lost: 94,796 customers (35.4%), one or two purchase days and none in the final 47+ days."*

### 3. Retention
- Heatmap: rows `cohort_week`, columns `weeks_since_first_purchase`, colour `retention_pct`. Leave out week 0 (always 100%), or it flattens the colour scale.
- Mark `is_first_cohort = True` with a border, a different colour or a label **on the chart**. Annotation text: *"First cohort inflated: it includes customers who were already shopping before the window began. Read retention from the second row down."*
- Mark `is_partial_week = True` cells, or filter them out with a note: *"Last column of each row covers only two days (data ends Tue, Sep 22)."*
- Headline: *"After week one, roughly 1 in 10 customers from each cohort buys again in any given week."*

### 4. Product performance
- Filter `category_performance.csv` to `meets_1000_unit_threshold = True`, and say so on the page.
- Two bars side by side, same category order: `units_sold` and `relative_revenue_scaled`. The point is to see Accessories and Shoes swap places.
- Scatter from `price_volume_by_article.csv`: `avg_price_scaled` against `units_sold`, one panel per category (or a category filter). A log scale on units helps with the long tail.
- Quintile line chart from `price_quintile_summary.csv`: mean and median units per quintile. Show both. The median decides where they disagree.
- Caption: *"Observational, not price elasticity. Different articles at different prices, not the same article repriced."*

### 5. What this data can't tell you
Text page, kept short. One line each:
- Prices are scaled, so there's no real revenue or margin.
- Three months is too short for true churn or lifetime retention.
- The first cohort is left-censored and reads too strong.
- 454 articles tie at the 10-unit floor, so worst sellers can't be named.
- Price-volume relationships differ by category, and a near-zero correlation hid an inverted U in Garment Full body.
- Significance is not strength: p < 0.0001 alongside r = -0.075.
